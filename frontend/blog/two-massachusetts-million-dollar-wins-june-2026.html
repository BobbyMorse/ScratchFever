$5 Million in 24 Hours, One Exit Apart on I-93

On Wednesday, June 18th, two of the Massachusetts scratch games ScratchFrenzy has been tracking for positive expected value cashed top-tier prizes — in towns four miles apart. The math said the edge was there. The data now says it landed.

Published June 19, 2026 · ScratchFrenzy Field Desk

Fabulous Fortune ($10) hit its $4,000,000 top prize at Red Apple in Braintree. Hours earlier, on the other side of the Blue Hills, $4,000,000 Bonus Loot ($10) paid out a $1,000,000 prize at Lucky Spot Convenience in Randolph. Same day. Same Mass. Lottery winners feed. Same two $10 games sitting near the top of the ScratchFrenzy MA EV board.

What the math said going in

Both of these games show up in the ScratchFrenzy expected-value table for Massachusetts. Here are the numbers from the most recent scrape of the MA Lottery's prizes-remaining data before the June 18th hits:

Fabulous Fortune has been one of the most extreme positive-EV $10 tickets on the ScratchFrenzy board for weeks — a 305% return on every dollar spent, by the published odds. It was also one of the tickets the ScratchFrenzy AI retailer caller had been actively phoning stores about for inventory checks.

$4,000,000 Bonus Loot sits in a different bucket — less extreme on EV, but with the largest prize pool remaining of any MA $10 ticket: nearly $26 million unclaimed, two $4M tops still on the board, and a far larger circulation. A $1M hit there is a textbook outcome from that pool.

Randolph and Braintree, four miles apart

Red Apple sits in Braintree, just off I-93. Lucky Spot Convenience sits in Randolph, the next town south. The fact that two of MA's most-tracked scratch games cashed seven-figure prizes within a half-tank of each other on the same day isn't strategy — it's variance. But it's the kind of variance that makes the case for actually looking at which games still have prizes left before you buy.

That's the whole pitch behind ScratchFrenzy: the state lotteries publish prizes-remaining data, and almost nobody reads it. We scrape it across every state we cover, run the EV math per dollar, and surface the games where the published numbers favor the player. Sometimes the math is ordinary. Sometimes the math has been screaming at you for a month, and then a Wednesday in June happens.
